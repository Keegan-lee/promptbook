# Sales Playbook and Voice guide — templates

Two binding documents that every sales routine reads on every run. The **Playbook** decides **what** may be said. The **Voice guide** decides **how** the sender says it. Draft them in Phase 3 of the kickoff, with the owner, from the brief, the public site, and the sender's sent-mail statistics. Then put each in its own shared document (one doc each) and record both URLs on the Outreach Settings row.

## Rules for both documents

- **Machine-readable.** Stable section numbers (routines cite `§3`, `§1.7`, `E4`). Labelled lines (`Pain:`, `Value:`, `Link:`, `Proof:`, `Gate:`, `Rule:`). Tables for data. Placeholders in square brackets: `[Name]`, `[Org]`, `[Town]`, `[booking link]`.
- **A version line near the top** (`Version X.Y · D Month YYYY`) and a **change log** at the end: `YYYY-MM-DD · vX.Y <change> (§refs) (<who>)`.
- **An exact END marker as the last line.** Routines check for it to detect a truncated read.
- **No emoji anywhere.** Some document export paths drop characters after each emoji, which can cut off the END marker. Write an emoji used in an exemplar as a word.
- **Under about 40,000 characters each.** Long documents get truncated on read. That limit is why the voice material is its own document.
- **Provenance in the text.** Each claim carries its source page in brackets. Each owner decision carries `([Owner], D Mon YYYY)`.
- **Only public claims.** The Playbook may only repeat what the live site states. If the document and the site disagree, the routine leaves the claim out and flags it.

## Template 1: Sales Playbook

```
SALES PLAYBOOK — [Company]

How this is used
Read in full on every run by: [list the sales routines]. Owner: [owner]. If this document
and the live site disagree, the routine leaves the claim out and flags it in its run summary.

How this changes
[Choose one, and say it here:]
(a) Only [owner] edits this document. Routines propose changes in their weekly digest.
(b) A weekly learning pass may edit it without approval, within these limits: it learns from the
    owner's edits to drafts (Owner Edited), skipped drafts, and reply, call, and booking rates by
    segment, territory, touch, and hook; at most 3 changes a week, each backed by 5 or more sends
    or 3 or more owner edits; §6 and §7 may only get stricter; no claim the live site doesn't
    state; never past 40,000 characters; every change logged with exact before and after text and
    its evidence, the version bumped, the change listed in the weekly digest, and revertible.

Version 1.0 · [D Month YYYY] ([status note, e.g. "draft; owner reviewing §2 and §7"])

1. Voice
How [sender] writes lives in the Voice guide: [link]. This playbook decides WHAT to say; the
Voice guide decides HOW. Notation: §n is a section of this document, §1.n and En are sections and
exemplars of the Voice guide. Exemplars teach voice only: this playbook's claims (§2) and
never-say list (§7) override anything an exemplar contains.

2. What we offer
The only facts a routine may state. One subsection per audience.

2.1 [Audience A]
- **[Label]:** [approved wording]. ([/source-page])
- **[Label]:** "[word-for-word quote]" — word for word. ([/source-page])
- **[Label]:** [wording] — only if asked. ([/source-page])
- **[Label]:** HOLD — not in email until [owner] clears it. [Fallback sentence, or "leave it out".]
- Gate: [any condition, e.g. "only to readers in [territory]"; or the fit rule: "pitch an
  individual only if [offer] exists within [radius] of them; check [live page] before every send"].
2.2 [Audience B] …
2.n Referral-out answers: [a regulated question the company refers out, to whom, and what never
    to say about it]. These are full answers, not holding drafts.

3. Segments
Proof: one true, generic sentence a reader can check, never a number.
Second link: the one extra link a segment may use besides the booking link.

[Supply side | Demand side | Organisations that bring customers]
**A. [Segment name] ([examples])**
- Pain: [one line]
- Value: [one line]
- Link: [https://… landing page]
- Proof: [one line]
- Gate: [optional scope or condition]
**B. …**

4. Sequences
Limits: at most [3] touches, at least [5] business days apart, one person per company while a
sequence runs, follow-ups in the same thread, opt-out and signature on every touch.

**Touch 1** (≤ [120] words). Purpose: one real reason from their own site, the segment's value
line, an optional hook, the ask. Include: [ … ]. Avoid: feature lists, numbers. Exemplar: E[n].
**Touch 2** (≤ [60] words, [5] BD later, same thread). Purpose: restate the ask and the link. One
new, true reason allowed. Avoid: guilt, a new pitch. Exemplar: E[n].
**Touch 3** (≤ [90] words, [5] BD after Touch 2, same thread). Purpose: one useful content link,
why it fits, a plain close that says this is the last note. Avoid: scarcity. Exemplar: E[n].
**Re-engage** ([90]+ days later, only with a genuinely new reason). New thread, the reason in the
first line, never mention the silence. Exemplar: E[n].
**Stalled sign-up check-in.** Describe what they started in general terms; offer help; never
reveal tracking or internal queues. Exemplar: E[n].
**Teammate pickup.** Fresh email, subject "Re: <their subject>", the teammate in cc, at most one
apology, the outstanding item answered first. Exemplar: E[n].

Replies (always drafts, never sent automatically):
- Interested → [action]. - Question → [action]. - Not now → [action].
- Not interested or unsubscribe → one line of thanks, no call to action, suppression list the
  same day.
- Referral → ask for an introduction with that person in cc; never cold-email them.

Referral programme wording (if any): [exact approved wording] or HOLD (gate: [condition]) with
fallback "[sentence]".

5. Call to action
- The booking link ends every email. It is read at run time ([settings override], then
  [source]); never typed, never a shared demo page.
- At most one other link, from §2, §3, or §9.
- Plain text only: no attachments, images, HTML, tracking pixels, link shorteners, or tracking
  parameters.
- CTA wording comes from the Voice guide §1.7.

6. Signature
- The sender's email-client signature exactly as it is (the mailer adds it; never type it).
- Opt-out line, just above the sign-off, on every outreach email: "[exact sentence]".
- Address line, outreach only (not replies): "[Company] · [mailing address]".
- Legal basis: [the anti-spam laws that apply]. Opt-outs are honoured the same day. Email only
  addresses a business publishes itself.

7. Never say
If a sentence might break one of these, cut it.
**About the company:** [live counts that drift] · [revenue, funding, investors] · [personnel] ·
[anything implying a guarantee, e.g. insurance coverage or a permit outcome] · …
**Site claims we don't repeat:** "[claim]" ([why]) · …
**How we write:** [banned phrases, e.g. "our system", automation tells] · …

8. Hooks policy
- Source: only Approved, unexpired rows in the Timely Hooks table.
- Fit: at most one per email, one sentence, relevant to the reader's audience and territory,
  never twice to the same person.
- Use it as approved: the Pitch Line word for word. No hook beats a forced one.

9. Content matching
- At most one piece per email, only when it fits the segment and territory. Published items
  only. Link it; never quote its figures. Never the same piece twice to one person.
- Excluded post types: [ … ].
- Starter mapping: 1. [slug] → segments [A, C]. 2. …
- Utility pages for Touch 3: [ … ].

10. Change log
YYYY-MM-DD · v1.0 First version from the kickoff interview ([owner])

END OF PLAYBOOK
```

## Template 2: Voice guide

Build the corpus statistics from the sender's sent mail (kickoff Phase 0, step 5). Keep counts and patterns only. Exemplars are rewritten with bracket placeholders so no real person, organisation, or detail survives.

```

this guide wins over any brand or social voice guide. Exemplars keep the sender's habits but
routines never copy typos or misspell a name. The Playbook's claims and never-say list override
anything shown in an exemplar.
Validation: [blind-test result, e.g. "the owner picked the real email in 3 of 6 pairs"], and
numbered, dated lessons from the owner's edits to drafts:
  (1) YYYY-MM-DD — [lesson].
```

## Checks before the owner approves v1.0

- Every §2 claim has a source page that states it today.
- Every segment has Pain, Value, Link, and Proof, and every Link resolves.
- §6 has the exact opt-out sentence and the address line from the brief.
- §7 includes every never-say seed from the brief.
- The Voice guide has an exemplar for every touch type the Playbook defines (Touch 3 and the stalled sign-up check-in are the ones most often missed).
- Exemplars break no §7 rule. Where the sender's real habits conflict with a Playbook cap, the Playbook says which wins.
- Neither document contains emoji, and both end with their END markers.
